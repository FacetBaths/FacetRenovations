The contract you sign determines everything that happens next. Period.

We see it constantly: Chicago homeowners who skip the hard questions because they don't want to seem difficult or because they're in a hurry to get started. Six weeks later, they're calling us to fix problems that never would have happened if they'd asked the right questions upfront.

This isn't about being adversarial. This is about protecting yourself and ensuring you're working with professionals who have nothing to hide. Companies with solid answers to these questions will respect you for asking. Companies that dodge or deflect... well, you'll find out who you're dealing with pretty quickly.

Here are the questions that separate professional renovation companies from contractors you'll regret hiring.

## Pricing and Payment Questions

### "Is this a fixed price or an estimate?"

**Why This Matters**: Estimates change. Fixed prices don't.

Here's what actually happens: contractor gives you an "estimate" of $18,000. Three weeks into the project, you're at $24,000 because of "unforeseen circumstances" and "necessary upgrades" that weren't in the original scope. You're already halfway through—what choice do you have?

**What Professional Companies Say**: "This is a fixed-price contract. The price includes everything we've discussed. If something truly unexpected comes up, we'll discuss it with you before proceeding, but our goal is to honor this price."

**Red Flags**:
- "Well, it's an estimate, things can change..."
- "We'll see what we find when we open up the walls"
- "There's always surprises in old Chicago homes"
- Refusing to commit to a fixed price for defined scope

**The Reality**: Professional renovation companies can provide fixed pricing because they know what they're doing. They've done enough bathrooms to anticipate issues. They build contingencies into their pricing instead of surprising you with change orders.

### "What's your payment schedule?"

**Why This Matters**: Payment schedules reveal how much risk you're taking on and whether the company has adequate cash flow.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "We typically take 10-30% deposit to order materials, progress payments tied to completion milestones, and final payment when you're completely satisfied."

**Red Flags**:
- Demanding 50%+ upfront
- Front-loading payments (asking for 70% before work is halfway done)
- "We need money for tools and insurance"
- Cash-only operations
- Asking for final payment before project completion

**Chicago Reality**: Legitimate renovation companies in Illinois don't need your money to buy tools or maintain insurance. They have working capital. If they're asking for massive upfront payments, they're either using your money to finish someone else's job or they're one bad week away from bankruptcy.

### "What happens if the project runs over budget or timeline?"

**Why This Matters**: This question reveals who takes responsibility when things go wrong.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "If we miss our timeline for reasons within our control, here's how we make it right [specific remedy]. If scope changes cause delays or cost increases, we'll get your approval in writing before proceeding."

**Red Flags**:
- Vague non-answers
- "These things happen"
- Blaming everything on suppliers or "old Chicago houses"
- No accountability mechanisms
- Suggesting overruns are normal and expected

## Timeline and Scheduling Questions

### "What's your guaranteed completion timeline?"

**Why This Matters**: The word "guaranteed" separates commitments from hopes.

Here's what actually happens: contractor says "about 3-4 weeks." Six weeks in, they're still not done. They blame suppliers, weather, other jobs. You've been brushing your teeth in the kitchen sink for two months. They have zero incentive to finish quickly because they already have your money.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "We guarantee completion in [X] weeks from start date. Here's our timeline with specific milestones. Here's what happens if we miss that timeline."

**Red Flags**:
- "Usually takes about..."
- "Depends on a lot of factors"
- Refusing to commit to specific dates
- "We'll get there when we get there"
- No consequences for missing timeline

**The Reality**: Professional renovation companies schedule one project at a time or have dedicated crews. They can commit to timelines because they're not juggling five jobs simultaneously.

### "Will you be working on other projects at the same time?"

**Why This Matters**: Determines whether you're the priority or one of many competing demands.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "We focus on one bathroom at a time, start to finish" OR "We have dedicated crews, so your project has its own team from start to finish."

**Red Flags**:
- "We have multiple jobs going"
- "We'll be back and forth between projects"
- Can't tell you who will be working on your project
- Evasive about crew availability

**Chicago-Specific Reality**: Chicago building inspections can cause delays. Ask how they handle inspection scheduling and whether inspection delays affect their timeline guarantee.

## Materials and Scope Questions

### "Exactly what materials are included at this price?"

**Why This Matters**: "Builder grade" and "standard" mean different things to different people.

We see it constantly: homeowner thinks they're getting quality fixtures. Contractor shows up with the cheapest possible materials. "Well, the contract just said 'toilet' and 'vanity'—these are toilets and vanities."

**What Professional Companies Say**: "Here's the specific product specifications, model numbers, and finishes included. We'll document your selections before ordering."

**What to Get in Writing**:
- Specific product names, manufacturers, model numbers
- Tile/wall surface material type and quality grade
- Fixture brands and finish options
- Flooring specifications
- Glass door specifications (thickness, type, coating)

**Red Flags**:
- "We use quality materials"
- "Builder grade" without further specification
- Refusing to provide specific product information
- "We'll figure that out later"

### "Who provides the materials—you or me?"

**Why This Matters**: Affects warranty coverage, timing, and who takes responsibility for defects or delays.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "We provide and warrant all materials. If there's a defect, we handle replacement at no cost to you" OR "If you want to source specific items yourself, here's how that affects our timeline and warranty."

**The Reality**: Professional companies typically source materials because they have trade accounts, can verify quality before installation, and can warranty the complete job. If they're asking you to source everything, they're not taking full responsibility.

### "What type of waterproofing system do you use?"

**Why This Matters**: This question immediately separates professionals from amateurs. In Chicago's climate with freeze-thaw cycles and humidity swings, waterproofing is critical.

**What Professional Companies Say**: Specific waterproofing system names (Schluter, RedGard, Kerdi, etc.) and where they apply them. Detailed explanation of their moisture management approach.

**Red Flags**:
- "We use plastic sheeting"
- "Cement board is waterproof" (it's not)
- Vague answers about "proper techniques"
- No specific product names
- "Never had a problem before"

**Chicago Reality**: Waterproofing failures show up 2-3 years later. By then, that cheap contractor isn't answering their phone. Professional companies use proven systems and stand behind them long-term.

## Licensing, Insurance, and Warranty Questions

### "Are you properly licensed and insured to work in Chicago?"

**Why This Matters**: Unlicensed contractors can't pull permits. Uninsured contractors make you liable if someone gets hurt.

**What to Verify**:
- General liability insurance (minimum $1M coverage)
- Workers compensation insurance
- Business registration with the State of Illinois
- Ability to pull permits with the City of Chicago

**What Professional Companies Say**: "Yes, here's our insurance certificate and business registration. We'll provide copies for your records."

**Red Flags**:
- "Insurance is expensive, but we're careful"
- "Small jobs don't need permits"
- Reluctance to provide documentation
- "We have insurance somewhere, I'll get you the info later"

**The Reality**: If an uninsured worker gets hurt on your property, you're liable. If work is done without permits and your house burns down, your insurance can deny the claim. This isn't optional paperwork—it's essential protection.

### "What warranty do you provide?"

**Why This Matters**: Anyone can promise quality work. Warranties prove they'll stand behind it.

**What Professional Companies Say**: Specific warranty terms—what's covered, for how long, and how issues get resolved. Workmanship warranties separate from manufacturer warranties.

**Minimum Acceptable**:
- Written warranty document
- Workmanship warranty (typically 1-5 years minimum)
- Clear process for warranty claims
- Company contact information that will remain valid

**Red Flags**:
- "We stand behind our work" (with no written warranty)
- Verbal promises only
- Warranty shorter than manufacturer's product warranty
- No clear warranty claim process

**The Reality**: Lifetime warranties on workmanship demonstrate serious confidence. One-year warranties are minimum acceptable. No written warranty means no accountability.

### "Can you provide references from recent Chicago projects?"

**Why This Matters**: Past performance predicts future results.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "Here are three recent bathroom projects in Chicago. I'll provide contact information if you'd like to speak with those homeowners directly."

**What to Ask References**:
- Did they hit their timeline?
- Did the final cost match the contract?
- How did they handle problems that came up?
- How was communication throughout the project?
- Would you hire them again?

**Red Flags**:
- Can't provide recent references
- Only references from years ago
- "People don't like to be bothered"
- Providing only online reviews (which can be faked)

## Process and Communication Questions

### "Who will be my main point of contact throughout the project?"

**Why This Matters**: Determines whether you'll get clear communication or play phone tag with multiple people who don't know your project details.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "I'll be your main contact" OR "Here's your project manager—they'll be on-site daily and available by phone/email."

**Red Flags**:
- No clear answer
- "Just call the main number"
- Different people for different questions
- Can't tell you who's responsible for what

### "How do you handle change orders?"

**Why This Matters**: Change orders are where costs spiral out of control if not managed properly.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "Any changes to scope require written approval with pricing before we proceed. No surprises."

**Red Flags**:
- Verbal change orders
- "We'll figure out the cost later"
- Pressuring you to approve changes immediately
- Not documenting scope changes

### "What happens if I'm not satisfied with the work?"

**Why This Matters**: Reveals commitment to customer satisfaction and conflict resolution approach.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "We do a walkthrough before final payment. If anything isn't right, we fix it. Your satisfaction is required before the project is considered complete."

**Red Flags**:
- "We've never had complaints"
- "Nothing we can do after it's installed"
- No clear resolution process
- Taking final payment before addressing concerns

## Chicago-Specific Questions

### "How do you handle permits and inspections?"

**Why This Matters**: Chicago requires permits for most bathroom renovations. Working without permits creates liability.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "We pull all required permits, schedule inspections, and ensure code compliance. Permit costs are [included in bid/separate line item]."

**Red Flags**:
- "Small jobs don't need permits"
- "Permits are a waste of money"
- "We can skip permits to save you money"
- No knowledge of Chicago permit requirements

**The Reality**: Unpermitted work can cause problems when you sell your home and creates insurance issues if something goes wrong. Professional companies handle permits as standard operating procedure.

### "How familiar are you with Chicago's older home challenges?"

**Why This Matters**: Chicago has unique housing stock—1920s bungalows, two-flats, graystones with specific challenges.

**What Professional Companies Say**: Specific examples of dealing with old plaster, knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing, structural quirks of Chicago buildings.

**Red Flags**:
- Generic answers that could apply anywhere
- No specific Chicago building knowledge
- Unfamiliarity with common local issues
- Recent arrival to the market with no local experience

### "What's your plan for material deliveries and parking?"

**Why This Matters**: Chicago parking, alleys, delivery access, and neighbor considerations affect project logistics.

**What Professional Companies Say**: Specific logistics plan for your property type. Discussion of dumpster placement, parking arrangements, material staging.

**Red Flags**:
- Haven't thought about logistics
- "We'll figure it out"
- No plan for material protection or site cleanup

## The Questions That Tell You Everything

### "Why should I choose your company?"

**Why This Matters**: The answer reveals whether they're selling on price, process, quality, or relationships.

**What Professional Companies Say**: Focus on process, guarantees, support, and outcomes. Specific differentiators backed by policies and practices.

**Red Flags**:
- "We're the cheapest"
- Can't articulate clear differentiators
- Bad-mouthing all other companies
- Making promises that sound too good to be true

### "What percentage of your projects come from referrals?"

**Why This Matters**: High referral rates indicate satisfied customers willing to stake their reputation on recommending the company.

**What Professional Companies Say**: "60-80% of our business comes from referrals and repeat customers."

**Red Flags**:
- Mostly new customer acquisition
- Reluctance to answer
- "We're growing so fast we haven't tracked that"

**The Reality**: Companies with high referral rates have to deliver good work—their reputation depends on it. Companies that constantly need new customers often burn bridges.

## How to Use These Questions

### Before the First Meeting
Research the company online, check reviews, verify licensing. Prepare your questions in writing.

### During the Estimate Meeting
Ask these questions directly. Take notes on answers. Professional companies won't be offended—they'll respect that you're being thorough.

### Comparing Proposals
Use answers to these questions as comparison criteria, not just price. The cheapest bid from someone who can't answer these questions will cost you more in the long run.

### Red Flag Threshold
If a company has trouble answering more than 2-3 of these questions clearly and confidently, keep looking. If they dodge or deflect on pricing, timeline, or warranty questions—walk away.

## What Professional Companies Bring to the Table

The companies that answer these questions well share common characteristics:

**Fixed-Price Contracts**: Because they can accurately estimate scope and costs

**Timeline Guarantees**: Because they control their schedule and crew availability

**Clear Warranties**: Because they stand behind their work long-term

**Proper Licensing and Insurance**: Because they operate as legitimate businesses

**Process-Driven Approach**: Because they've done enough projects to systematize quality

**Ongoing Support**: Because they view customer relationships as long-term, not transactional

## Key Takeaways

The questions you ask before signing determine whether your bathroom renovation is smooth or becomes a cautionary tale you share at dinner parties.

Professional renovation companies welcome these questions. They have clear, confident answers because they operate with systems, accountability, and genuine commitment to customer satisfaction.

Contractors who dodge these questions, make vague promises, or pressure you to sign quickly—they're showing you exactly who they are. Believe them.

The best renovation experiences come from homeowners who ask hard questions upfront and choose companies that provide transparent, documented, specific answers.

Your bathroom renovation will take 2-3 months and cost $15,000-$65,000. Spending two hours asking the right questions is the best investment you can make in that process.

Don't sign anything until you have clear, written, specific answers to every one of these questions.
